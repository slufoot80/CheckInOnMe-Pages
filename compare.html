Minimal data vs. a useful alert

Demumu takes a minimal approach: its listing says it avoids location permissions entirely. That keeps things simple, but it also means the email your contact receives can say you've gone quiet — not where you are. SafePulse makes the opposite trade-off on purpose: when you miss a check-in, the alert includes your last known location, because in an emergency “where?” is the first question.

SafePulse still has no accounts, no ads, and no tracking. What leaves your device is only what an alert needs — your name, your contacts' numbers and emails, and your last known location — used to deliver that alert, never sold, and deleted when you delete your data.

Demumu and “Are You Dead?” questions

What people ask when they're looking for a check-in app like Demumu.

What is Demumu?

Demumu is a daily check-in app for people who live alone, made by a small team in China. You tap once a day to say you're okay, and if you miss check-ins for consecutive days, it emails one emergency contact. It went viral in China in January 2026 under the name “Are You Dead?” (死了么, Sileme) before rebranding as Demumu for its global launch.

Is Demumu the same app as “Are You Dead?”

Yes. “Are You Dead?” was the app's original English name. Its developer renamed it Demumu in January 2026, saying the new name would make it easier to expand globally, so news coverage of the viral “Are You Dead?” app is about the same product.

What is a good alternative to Demumu?

SafePulse is a check-in app built on the same idea as Demumu, but it alerts people sooner and tells them where you are. Instead of a once-a-day tap, you set a timer anywhere from 30 minutes to 48 hours. If you miss it, SafePulse warns you first, then texts and emails up to 10 trusted contacts with your last known location — even if your phone is dead or switched off.

Is there a Demumu app for Android?

Check the developer name before you download or pay for any Android app called Demumu. As of September 2026, a Google Play listing using that name is published by a different company than the one behind the viral app. SafePulse is available on Google Play, the Samsung Galaxy Store and Huawei AppGallery, as well as on iPhone and Amazon Fire tablets.

Does Demumu send my location to my emergency contact?

No. Demumu's app listing says it avoids location permissions, so its alert email can tell your contact you've gone quiet, but not where you are. SafePulse includes your last known location in every alert, because in an emergency the first thing your contacts need to know is where to go.

Is SafePulse related to Demumu or the “Are You Dead?” app?

No. SafePulse is built by an independent developer in Michigan and has no connection to Demumu or its developer. SafePulse was published on Google Play under the name “Are You Dead?” before it was renamed SafePulse.

How much does SafePulse cost compared to Demumu?

Demumu is cheaper: news reports put it at about $1 as a paid download. SafePulse is $2.99 a month on iPhone and a one-time $4.99 on Google Play, the Samsung Galaxy Store and Amazon. The difference pays for text message alerts, your location in every alert, SOS, and a cloud backstop that alerts your contacts even when your phone is off.
